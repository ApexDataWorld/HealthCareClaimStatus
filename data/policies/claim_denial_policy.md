# Claim Denial Policy

## Overview

This policy outlines the circumstances under which health insurance claims may be partially or fully denied, and the procedures for members to appeal such denials.

## 1. Non-Covered Services

### 1.1 Definition
Services that are not included in the member's health plan benefit structure are considered non-covered services. These services will be denied in full unless the member is subject to a specific rider that includes coverage for such services.

### 1.2 Common Non-Covered Services
The following services are typically non-covered under standard plans:
- Cosmetic procedures (unless medically necessary for reconstruction following injury or illness)
- Experimental or investigational treatments not approved by the FDA
- Weight loss programs and bariatric surgery (unless medically necessary per guidelines)
- Fertility treatments including assisted reproductive technology (unless specifically included in plan)
- Acupuncture (unless specifically included in plan rider)
- Chiropractic care beyond specified limits
- Routine eye examinations and eyeglasses (separate vision plans apply)
- Dental services (unless included in separate dental plan)

### 1.3 Denial Code: CO-50
Claims for non-covered services will be denied under CARC code CO-50 "Non-covered service or item" with the explanation that "This charge is not covered under the patient's health plan."

## 2. Prior Authorization Requirements

### 2.1 Services Requiring Prior Authorization
Certain services must receive prior authorization before being rendered. Failure to obtain prior authorization will result in claim denial.

### 2.2 Services Requiring Prior Authorization
The following services require prior authorization:
- Inpatient hospital admissions (except emergency admissions)
- Outpatient surgical procedures with estimated cost exceeding $5,000
- Diagnostic imaging including MRI, CT scans
- Specialty care referrals (specific to HMO plans)
- Physical therapy courses exceeding 12 visits
- Mental health inpatient treatment
- Durable medical equipment with total cost exceeding $1,000

### 2.3 Failure to Obtain Authorization
If prior authorization was not obtained before service delivery, claims will be denied under CARC code CO-197 "Precertification/authorization absent" with the message "Service requires prior authorization which was not obtained."

### 2.4 Member Responsibility
Members are responsible for understanding authorization requirements before obtaining services. Providers may issue a Notice of Non-Coverage (NNOC) or Advance Beneficiary Notice (ABN) before service delivery to inform members of potential non-coverage.

## 3. Out-of-Network Providers

### 3.1 Network Status Requirements
Services rendered by providers who do not maintain a network agreement with the plan are subject to different benefit levels. HMO plans do not cover out-of-network services except in emergencies.

### 3.2 PPO Plans
PPO members may use out-of-network providers but will receive reduced benefits. Claims will be paid at a lower percentage (e.g., 60% vs. 80% for in-network).

### 3.3 Denial for Out-of-Network Usage
If a member uses an out-of-network provider without authorization (in an HMO plan), the claim will be denied under CARC code CO-222 "Out-of-network provider" with the explanation "Services by non-contracted provider not covered as in-network."

## 4. Medical Necessity

### 4.1 Definition
Services must be medically necessary to be covered. Medical necessity is defined as a service or procedure that is:
- Appropriate for the diagnosis or treatment condition
- Consistent with evidence-based clinical guidelines
- Not primarily for convenience
- Consistent with the standard of care

### 4.2 Utilization Review
Claims may be subject to utilization review to determine medical necessity. Services that fail to meet medical necessity criteria will be denied.

### 4.3 Denial Code: CO-80
Claims denied due to insufficient documentation of medical necessity will be denied under CARC code CO-80 "Claim denied or reduced based on information provider submitted" with the message "Documentation insufficient to support charges."

### 4.4 Common Reasons for Non-Medical Necessity Denial
- Multiple tests for the same condition within a short timeframe
- Treatment duration exceeding evidence-based guidelines
- Services inconsistent with patient's age or condition
- Treatment without documented clinical justification

## 5. Timely Filing

### 5.1 Filing Deadline
All claims must be submitted within 180 days of the date of service. Claims submitted after this deadline will be denied.

### 5.2 Denial Code: CO-29
Claims submitted after the timely filing deadline will be denied under CARC code CO-29 "Time limit for filing has expired" with the message "Claim was not submitted within the required time period."

### 5.3 Exceptions
Emergency situations may allow for extended filing periods. Provider appeals with documentation of extenuating circumstances may be considered.

## 6. Frequency Limitations

### 6.1 Service Frequency Limits
Certain services are limited to specific frequency. Services beyond these limits will be denied.

### 6.2 Common Frequency Limits
- Physical therapy: 30 visits per plan year
- Chiropractic care: 20 visits per plan year
- Mental health outpatient: 52 visits per plan year (1 per week)
- Preventive care visits: 2 per plan year for annual wellness
- Diagnostic imaging: Limited based on medical necessity

### 6.3 Denial Code: CO-156
Claims exceeding frequency limits will be denied under CARC code CO-156 "Claim frequency exceeded" with the message "Service frequency limit for this service has been reached."

## 7. Coordination of Benefits

### 7.1 Primary vs. Secondary Coverage
When a member has multiple sources of coverage, benefits are coordinated to prevent duplicate payments. The plan will not pay more than its normal benefit amount.

### 7.2 Claim Denial for COB
Claims may be denied or reduced when another insurance plan is the primary payer. Members must report all coverage to ensure proper coordination.

## 8. Appeal Rights

### 8.1 Member Appeal Rights
Members have the right to appeal denied claims within 180 days of the denial notice. Appeals must include:
- Written explanation of why the denial is incorrect
- Supporting medical documentation
- Any new information relevant to the claim

### 8.2 Appeal Process
1. Member submits written appeal to Claims Department
2. Claims Department conducts initial review (5-10 business days)
3. If denied at initial level, request independent external review
4. External reviewer conducts independent medical review
5. Decision communicated in writing within timeframes per state law

### 8.3 Appeal Decisions
Appeals for non-covered services (non-appealable denials) will be upheld. Other denials may be overturned if new clinical evidence supports medical necessity.

## 9. Specific Denial Scenarios

### 9.1 Duplicate Claims
If the same service is submitted twice, the duplicate claim will be denied under CARC code CO-18 "Duplicate claim/service" with the message "This claim or service has already been processed and paid."

### 9.2 Bundled Services
Services that are bundled into a global fee will not be paid separately. Component claims will be denied under CARC code CO-7 "The benefit for this service is included in the payment for another service."

### 9.3 Missing Diagnosis Code
Claims without valid diagnosis codes to justify medical necessity will be denied under CARC code CO-33 "Claim submitted with no valid/qualifying diagnosis code."

### 9.4 Referral Requirements
HMO members requiring referrals for specialist care who fail to obtain referral will have claims denied under CARC code CO-42 "Claim submitted without referral."

## 10. Claim Status Communication

### 10.1 Denial Notice Requirements
Claim denials must be communicated in writing within 30 days of determination, including:
- Reason for denial with specific policy references
- CARC/RARC codes used
- Appeal rights and procedures
- Contact information for appeals

### 10.2 Member Dispute Rights
Members may dispute any claim decision through our appeals process. Disputes must be documented in writing with supporting evidence.
