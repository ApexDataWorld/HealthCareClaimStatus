# Medical Necessity Guidelines

## 1. Medical Necessity Definition

### 1.1 Core Definition
Medical necessity is defined as a service, supply, treatment, or procedure that is:
- Appropriate for the member's diagnosed condition or symptom
- Consistent with established clinical guidelines and standards of care
- Not primarily for the convenience of the member or provider
- Not duplicative of services already provided
- Consistent with the evidence base for the specific condition

### 1.2 Standards Used
- Evidence-based clinical guidelines (NCCN, ACP, ADA, etc.)
- FDA approvals and warnings
- Peer-reviewed medical literature
- Medicare coverage determination standards
- State medical society standards of care

### 1.3 Who Determines Medical Necessity
- Plan medical reviewers (MDs, RNs, specialists)
- Independent external reviewers (when appealed)
- Treating provider (initial determination)
- Utilization management program

## 2. Diagnostic Services

### 2.1 Laboratory Tests
Medical necessity criteria for lab tests:
- Ordered by licensed physician based on clinical findings
- Appropriate for diagnosis, treatment monitoring, or screening
- Consistent with accepted standards of care
- Not duplicate testing without clinical justification
- Frequency appropriate for condition

### 2.2 Imaging Services
Medical necessity for imaging (X-ray, CT, MRI, ultrasound):
- Clinical indication documented in medical record
- Appropriate modality for clinical question
- Not routine screening unless specified benefit
- Follow American College of Radiology (ACR) appropriateness criteria
- Prior authorization required for MRI, CT, advanced imaging

### 2.3 Genetic Testing
- Ordered by physician for documented indication
- Medical history consistent with positive test likelihood
- Results will affect treatment decision
- Test approved by FDA or CLIA certified laboratory
- Cost-benefit analysis supports testing

## 3. Surgical Services

### 3.1 Operative Indication
Surgery is medically necessary when:
- Clinical indication clearly documented
- Conservative treatments attempted or considered inappropriate
- Risk/benefit analysis favorable
- Procedure appropriate for condition severity
- Surgeon qualifications appropriate for procedure type

### 3.2 Common Surgical Approval Criteria

#### Hip/Knee Replacement
- Severe osteoarthritis with pain limiting function
- Failed conservative treatment (PT, weight loss, anti-inflammatories)
- X-ray confirmation of moderate-to-severe arthritis
- Joint replacement indicated per clinical guidelines

#### Cataract Surgery
- Visual acuity reduction affecting function
- Age not a contraindication
- Posterior capsule opacification may warrant repeat surgery
- Prior authorization required

#### Spinal Surgery
- MRI confirmation of spinal pathology
- Symptoms consistent with imaging findings
- Conservative treatment period documented (typically 6-12 weeks)
- Neurologic deficit or myelopathy present
- Prior authorization required for fusion and complex procedures

#### Bariatric Surgery
- BMI > 40 or BMI > 35 with comorbidities
- Documented failed weight loss attempts
- Psychological evaluation completed
- Surgical center accreditation required
- Prior authorization required

### 3.3 Cosmetic vs. Reconstructive
- Cosmetic procedures not covered (rhinoplasty for appearance, face lift)
- Reconstructive procedures covered (following injury, disease, cancer)
- Breast reconstruction after mastectomy: Covered
- Post-surgical revisions for functional improvement: Covered
- Aesthetic improvement alone: Not covered

## 4. Medications and Biologics

### 4.1 Prescription Drug Necessity
Medication is medically necessary when:
- Prescribed by licensed healthcare provider
- Approved by FDA for specific indication
- Not contraindicated for member's conditions
- No more cost-effective equivalent available
- Therapeutic class appropriate for diagnosis

### 4.2 Prior Authorization for Specialty Drugs
Specialty medications (typically high-cost biologics) require:
- Clinical indication documentation
- Contraindication screening
- Failure of lower-cost alternatives (if applicable)
- Concurrent conditions management plan
- Patient monitoring plan

### 4.3 Off-Label Use
Off-label medication use is covered when:
- Supported by peer-reviewed medical literature
- Referenced in established clinical guidelines (NCCN, etc.)
- Represents standard of care for condition
- Prior authorization required
- Documentation of clinical justification

### 4.4 Medication Combinations
- Drug-drug interactions must be managed
- Appropriateness of multi-drug regimens reviewed
- Duplication within same therapeutic class denied
- Alternative agents considered for cost-effectiveness

## 5. Rehabilitation Services

### 5.1 Physical Therapy Necessity
PT is medically necessary when:
- Specific diagnosis with therapeutic indication
- Functional limitation objectively documented
- Specific goals established (ROM, strength, mobility improvement)
- Skilled professional intervention required
- Member actively participating in program

### 5.2 PT Frequency Limits
- Typical authorization: 12 visits in 30 days
- Extended authorization available for complex cases
- Must demonstrate progress objectively
- Discharge when goals achieved or plateau reached

### 5.3 Occupational Therapy
- Similar criteria as physical therapy
- Focus on activities of daily living
- Work re-entry or return-to-work goals
- 30-visit annual limit applies

### 5.4 Speech-Language Pathology
- Documented speech, language, or swallowing deficit
- Skilled assessment and treatment needed
- Realistic goals for improvement
- Progress documented at regular intervals

## 6. Mental Health Services

### 6.1 Outpatient Mental Health
- Diagnosis from DSM-5 or ICD-10
- Licensed mental health provider
- Treatment plan with specific goals
- Individual or group therapy appropriate
- Frequency appropriate for condition severity

### 6.2 Inpatient Psychiatric Care
Medical necessity requires:
- Acute psychiatric crisis or danger
- Hospitalization necessary for evaluation/treatment
- Less restrictive settings inappropriate
- Active treatment plan in place
- Daily physician oversight
- Discharge planning initiated at admission

### 6.3 Substance Abuse Treatment
- Diagnosis of substance use disorder
- Treatment level appropriate for severity
- Integrated medical/psychiatric care
- Medication-assisted treatment for opioid use
- Continuing care/recovery support plan

## 7. Preventive Services

### 7.1 Screening Tests
Covered preventive screenings must be:
- Age-appropriate per USPSTF or ACS guidelines
- Risk-stratified (higher risk for earlier/frequent screening)
- FDA-approved methodology
- Supported by clinical evidence
- Part of established screening program

### 7.2 Immunizations
- CDC-recommended schedule
- Age and risk group appropriate
- Necessary for specified outbreak circumstances
- Allergy/contraindication assessment completed
- Documentation of previous immunization history

### 7.3 Counseling Services
Preventive counseling covered includes:
- Smoking cessation counseling
- Alcohol misuse screening and brief intervention
- Weight management counseling
- Depression screening
- Annual wellness visit

## 8. Frequency and Duration Limitations

### 8.1 Appropriate Frequencies
Service frequency determined by:
- Diagnosis and condition severity
- Therapeutic modality and expected improvement timeline
- Clinical guidelines for condition
- Individual response to treatment
- Plateau of improvement reached

### 8.2 Long-Term Treatment
Services appropriate for ongoing management:
- Chronic disease management (diabetes, hypertension, COPD)
- Mental health ongoing care for chronic conditions
- Maintenance physical therapy for neurologic conditions
- Continued medication therapy

### 8.3 Service Discontinuation
Services should be discontinued when:
- Goals achieved
- Functional plateau reached without improvement
- Medical condition stability established
- Transition to maintenance care appropriate
- Member achieving self-management

## 9. Documentation Requirements

### 9.1 Required Documentation
For medical necessity determination:
- Chief complaint and diagnosis
- Objective clinical findings
- Pertinent past medical history
- Current medications affecting condition
- Treatment plan with specific goals
- Expected outcome and timeline
- Provider credentials and qualifications

### 9.2 Records Review
Plan may request:
- Complete medical record excerpts
- Additional history or physical exam details
- Imaging or laboratory results
- Correspondence between providers
- Prior treatment records

### 9.3 Peer-to-Peer Review
- Treating provider may request peer-to-peer discussion
- Medical reviewer speaks directly with treating provider
- Opportunity to present clinical rationale
- Common for appeals or complex cases

## 10. Denial Management

### 10.1 Denial for Lack of Medical Necessity
Denials based on medical necessity include:
- Insufficient documentation of clinical indication
- Diagnosis inconsistent with treatment modality
- Condition severity insufficient for recommended treatment
- Service duration/frequency excessive
- Alternative lower-cost option appropriate

### 10.2 Appeal Process
- Member may appeal within 180 days
- New clinical evidence may support medical necessity
- Independent external review available
- IMR reviewer assesses medical necessity de novo

### 10.3 Exceptions Process
- Exceptional cases may be approved despite policy limitations
- Medical director review of exception requests
- Documented medical justification required
- Approval decision within 10 business days

## 11. Condition-Specific Guidelines

### 11.1 Diabetes Management
- Glucose monitoring: Appropriate frequency for glycemic control
- Medications: Per ADA guidelines for HbA1c target
- Retinal screening: Annual for type 1, biennial for type 2
- Nephropathy screening: Annual urine microalbumin
- Neuropathy assessment: Monofilament testing annually

### 11.2 Hypertension
- Diagnosis requires multiple BP measurements
- Treatment modality based on comorbidities
- Frequency of BP monitoring per guidelines
- Secondary hypertension workup appropriate for presentation

### 11.3 Depression
- Diagnosis requires documented symptoms per DSM-5
- Treatment modality matched to severity
- Psychotherapy: 4-6 weeks before medication change
- Medication: At least 6-week trial at therapeutic dose
- Hospitalization for suicidal ideation with plan/intent

### 11.4 Obesity
- Weight loss counseling: Part of preventive care
- Medication: FDA-approved agents with documented indication
- Surgery: BMI criteria and failed medical management
- Lifestyle modification programs covered

## 12. High-Cost Procedures

### 12.1 Joint Replacement Appropriateness
- Severity of degenerative joint disease
- Failed conservative therapy documentation
- Functional limitations affecting daily activities
- Radiographic confirmation of severe arthritis
- Surgeon experience with procedure type

### 12.2 Spinal Fusion Criteria
- MRI or CT confirmation of spinal pathology
- Symptoms consistent with imaging findings
- Neurologic deficit or progressive neurologic compromise
- Conservative treatment period (6-12 weeks typical)
- Non-fusion alternatives considered and deemed inappropriate

### 12.3 Cardiac Procedures
- Cardiac catheterization: Appropriate clinical indication
- Stenting: Appropriate lesion characteristics and symptoms
- CABG: Left main disease or multi-vessel disease with EF < 40%
- Device implantation: Ejection fraction and arrhythmia criteria

## 13. Quality and Outcome Measures

### 13.1 Outcome Tracking
Plan tracks medical necessity outcomes:
- Admission/readmission rates
- Complication rates
- Functional improvement measures
- Patient satisfaction scores
- Cost per outcome comparison

### 13.2 Guideline Updates
- Annual review of medical necessity criteria
- Incorporation of new clinical guidelines
- Removal of outdated standards
- Evidence-based decision making

### 13.3 Appeal Success Rates
- Tracking of successful medical necessity appeals
- Analysis of appeal reversal reasons
- Process improvement based on appeal patterns
